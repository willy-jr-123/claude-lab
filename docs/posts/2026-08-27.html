CIA chief made secret Moscow trip to warn Russia against attacking NATO's Baltic states

CIA Director John Ratcliffe made an undisclosed trip to Moscow on August 25, his first known visit to Russia since taking the post, primarily to warn Russian officials against any military action against NATO members, with US officials particularly concerned about a limited incursion or cyberattack targeting Estonia, Latvia or Lithuania amid mounting pressure on President Vladimir Putin over the war in Ukraine. Ratcliffe also pressed Moscow to curb its military and economic support for Iran and cautioned against "overreacting" to expanded US sanctions targeting countries that maintain ties with Tehran. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Ratcliffe did not meet Putin directly, saying contacts "took place through the intelligence services."

Source: CNN, RFE/RL

Iran and Oman claim Hormuz revenue-sharing deal as dispute over its finality emerges

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it has reached an agreement with Oman on sharing control of the Strait of Hormuz and splitting revenue from transiting vessels, converting an earlier temporary shipping corridor into a formal arrangement; Tehran says the deal would bar US and Israeli vessels and fine other violators up to 20% of cargo value. A senior Iranian source later told Reuters the agreement "has not been finalized," underscoring continued uncertainty over the fate of the vital oil chokepoint nearly six months into the war between Iran and the US and Israel.

Source: Bloomberg

Gaza ceasefire enters "engineering table" phase as Board of Peace envoy faults Israeli strikes

Nikolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace's high representative overseeing Gaza's ceasefire, told the UN Security Council that President Trump's 20-point peace plan has not stalled but has moved from "the negotiating table to the engineering table" as implementation details are worked out with Israel. He said Hamas has agreed to disarm and hand Gaza to civilian rule, but continuing Israeli strikes — including one that killed a person in a tent in Khan Younis on August 26 — mean Gazans still cannot feel the war is truly over. Mladenov said a recent meeting with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Trump adviser Jared Kushner narrowed remaining "technical" disagreements.

Source: NPR

Canada-US trade war deepens as Carney readies retaliatory tariffs

Trade talks between Canada and the United States collapsed after Washington imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, including autos, hockey sticks, building materials and clothing. Prime Minister Mark Carney has suspended further negotiations and says Canada will match the tariffs "dollar for dollar" on about $27.6 billion of US goods starting September 8, likening the dispute to being "attacked." US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer blamed the breakdown on Canada, saying it made new demands and walked back commitments agreed to earlier.

Source: NPR, NBC News

Russia launches massive missile and drone barrage on Ukraine, hitting Odesa and Zaporizhzhia

Russia struck Ukraine overnight with two Oniks anti-ship cruise missiles, an unspecified number of ballistic missiles and 258 drones, targeting Kyiv along with port, grain, energy and industrial infrastructure, Ukrainian officials said. Ukraine's air force said nearly 90% of the drones were shot down along with one Oniks missile and seven ballistic missiles, though the attack on Odesa lasted through the night, damaging a school and a multi-story building and injuring three people; at least seven people, including three children, were hurt in Zaporizhzhia. President Volodymyr Zelensky thanked European allies for new air-defense commitments made this week.

Source: Free Malaysia Today

Sudanese paramilitary attacks displace hundreds more near El Obeid

Fresh attacks by the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces on villages in North Kordofan forced hundreds more residents to flee toward El Obeid, the region's besieged capital, extending a wave of displacement that has already pushed more than 200,000 people from their homes across Kordofan since fighting intensified late last year. The attacks come even as Sudan's army presses recent battlefield gains in the region and a growing number of RSF fighters and commanders defect, a sign the conflict is fragmenting rather than nearing resolution.

Source: Sudan Tribune
