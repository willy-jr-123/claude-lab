Trump vows to "hit Iran hard" after weekend strikes on US bases in Jordan

President Trump said the United States will respond forcefully after Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed missile and drone strikes on US-linked facilities at Jordan's King Hussein and Al Azraq air bases over the weekend, the first major clash between the two sides in a month. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran would answer any US strike with a response "dozens of times greater," raising fears of a fresh escalatory spiral six months into the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz. Oil prices jumped on the news, with Brent crude climbing above $91 a barrel.

Source: Al Jazeera

Russian barrage kills 12 in Kyiv region for sixth straight night of strikes

Russia bombarded Kyiv and its suburbs for more than 12 hours overnight into Tuesday, killing at least 12 people and wounding 21 in the sixth consecutive day of attacks on the Ukrainian capital. Four of the dead were in the suburb of Boryspil, where a residential building was hit, while seven more were killed at a railway depot on Kyiv's left bank. Ukraine's air force said the assault used ballistic and cruise missiles alongside 218 drones and decoys, about a third of them harder-to-intercept jet-powered models.

Source: Bloomberg, France 24

Germany blames Russia for foiled explosive-drone plot at Leipzig airport

Germany's interior ministry said Tuesday that Russia's military intelligence service, the GRU, was behind a plot involving explosive-laden drones discovered in and around Leipzig/Halle airport in early August. One drone carrying military-grade explosives was found inside the airport's secure area, while a separate flying object struck a cargo plane operated by Ukraine's Antonov Airlines, causing minor damage before the aircraft landed safely in Hannover. Leipzig/Halle is one of Europe's busiest cargo hubs and has served as Antonov's base since Russia destroyed its home airfield in 2022.

Source: CNN

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll passes 1,000 as search for missing continues

The combined death toll from last week's Himalayan glacial-lake outburst flood has crossed 1,000, with 987 confirmed dead in Nepal and 16 in Tibet. Rescue teams have pulled at least 11,814 people to safety, but hundreds remain missing, particularly in Nepal's northern Rasuwa district along the Tibetan border, where the flooding first struck on August 26. The disaster began when a glacial collapse sent rock, ice and meltwater surging into the Trishuli River valley, burying buildings, bridges and roads downstream.

Source: Al Jazeera, Euronews

Israeli-backed raid to seize Hamas security chief kills four in Gaza City

Israeli strikes covering the withdrawal of an Israeli-backed raid in western Gaza City killed four people on Tuesday, including three children and a woman, according to Gaza's civil defense agency. Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz confirmed the operation, saying forces had arrested a senior Hamas figure identified as the head of the group's internal security service, and that Israel would keep pursuing Hamas members "at all times." The raid took place amid the US-backed ceasefire that took effect in October, which has curbed large-scale fighting but not halted Israeli strikes inside Gaza.

Source: Al-Monitor, The Times of Israel

Niger says it repelled coup attempt as dozens of soldiers arrested

Niger's military-led government said it put down an attempted coup after sustained gunfire and explosions broke out across parts of the capital, Niamey, on Saturday. Soldiers identifying with the "Armed Forces for the Restoration of the Fatherland" attacked the main international airport, briefly seized state radio and television facilities, and moved toward the presidential palace before loyal security forces regained control. Dozens of soldiers believed to be young officers opposed to President Abdourahamane Tiani's rule have since been arrested.

Source: Al Jazeera
