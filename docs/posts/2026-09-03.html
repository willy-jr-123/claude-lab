Russia strikes Kyiv Coca-Cola plant on eighth straight day of attacks on the capital

Russian drones and missiles hit Kyiv for an eighth consecutive day on Thursday, damaging a Coca-Cola bottling plant and injuring at least four people, including a 12-year-old girl. The overnight barrage also struck one of Ukraine's largest pharmaceutical warehouses near the capital and a postal depot in Dnipro, killing one person, while missiles hit the Kharkiv region and injured about ten more, some of them children. Ukraine's air force said it downed most of the incoming drones and missiles but could not stop all of them from reaching their targets.

Source: Kyiv Independent

Israel begins releasing five Lebanese detainees in US-brokered goodwill gesture

Israel handed over the first of five Lebanese detainees it agreed to release as a "gesture of goodwill" toward Lebanon, with the man transferred to the Lebanese army via the Red Cross on Thursday. The release follows the seventh round of US-mediated Israel-Lebanon talks held in Rome in August, and came after Lebanon assisted Israel in searching for the remains of Jewish community leaders abducted and killed during Lebanon's civil war. The freed detainees are civilians with no affiliation to Hezbollah who were held during recent fighting in southern Lebanon.

Source: The Times of Israel, The National

Trump floats renaming Strait of Hormuz "Trump Strait" amid ongoing Iran standoff

President Trump suggested on social media Wednesday that the Strait of Hormuz be renamed "Trump Strait," writing that "now that we have it under U.S.A. control" the waterway would be "hotter than ever before." Asked at the White House later whether he was serious, Trump downplayed the idea, saying "it was just thrown out there." The proposal came days after Trump ordered Lake Ontario renamed "Lake America" amid a trade dispute with Canada, and underscores the continuing US-Iran contest for control of the strait, through which roughly a fifth of global oil transited before the war.

Source: CNBC, Forbes

Israel says it is ready to "decisively" strike Iran if attacked during Jewish holidays

Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel's military has completed preparations to "respond with great force" if Iran attacks during the upcoming Jewish high holiday season, which begins in mid-September, noting Iran has a history of timing attacks around Jewish holidays. Katz said Israel's air force and intelligence directorate have readied both defensive systems and counterattack plans "independently of anyone else" should hostilities with Tehran flare again. The warning comes amid persistently high tension between Israel, the US and Iran following months of intermittent strikes.

Source: The Times of Israel

UN report says foreign networks, including Colombian mercenaries, are fueling Sudan's war

A UN fact-finding mission reported Thursday that foreign weapons, technology, fighters and logistics networks are sustaining Sudan's civil war and expanding the warring parties' ability to strike distant civilian areas with drones. Investigators said they have grounds to believe a network involving individuals and entities in the UAE, Chad, Libya and Somalia supplied the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces with weapons and training, and that up to 2,000 Colombian contractors helped operate drones and other advanced weapons for the RSF. The UAE denied involvement, Somalia said it was taking the allegations seriously, and Colombia said it opposed mercenary activity. The UN says the war has now displaced 11.8 million people and left 24.6 million facing acute hunger.

Source: The Washington Post

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll climbs past 1,250 as thousands remain missing

The death toll from last month's Himalayan glacial-lake outburst flood rose to 1,252 in Nepal, up from 1,114 a day earlier, with about 4,216 people still listed as missing there. Chinese state media reported an additional 16 deaths and 546 missing on the Tibetan side, bringing the combined toll to 1,268 dead and 4,762 missing. Nearly 21,500 security personnel remain mobilized in the search-and-rescue effort, which has rescued close to 12,000 people since a glacial collapse sent rock, ice and meltwater surging into the Trishuli River valley in late August.

Source: ABC News
