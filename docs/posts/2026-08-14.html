August 14, 2026

Ukraine declares Dnipropetrovsk counteroffensive a success as Russia intensifies missile strikes

Kyiv announced the effective end of an eight-month counteroffensive in the Oleksandrivka sector, saying it recaptured 745 square kilometers (287 square miles) across Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia and Donetsk oblasts and pushed Russian forces back over the regional border. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the operation killed nearly 10,000 Russian soldiers and wounded more than 6,600. Russia responded by intensifying ballistic and missile attacks on Ukrainian cities, and Moscow has rejected recent ceasefire proposals while threatening tougher strikes.

Source: Al Jazeera, The Kyiv Independent

Strait of Hormuz traffic grinds to a near standstill as US warns of deeper economic pressure on Iran

Vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz slowed further after two more ships were attacked in the waterway, with U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth saying Washington can maintain its naval blockade of Iran "indefinitely." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. is preparing to shift its campaign toward financial measures, describing plans for "economic isolation like the world has never seen before." A senior Iranian source said talks to build on a June agreement to end the war have made no progress, and Iran has resumed strikes on ships it says are transiting the strait without permission.

Source: The Times of Israel, CNBC

Israeli strike kills another Hamas commander in Gaza amid fragile ceasefire

The Israeli military said a strike in Khan Younis killed Hudhaifa Khaled Suleiman Qawari, described as a commander in Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade who had been training fighters, moving weapons and "advancing terror attacks" against Israeli troops. It follows a strike a day earlier that killed another Hamas commander in Gaza City. The nominal ceasefire in place since October has remained fragile, with rights groups reporting more than 1,250 Palestinians killed and over 4,100 wounded since it began, even as the weekly death toll has fallen sharply in recent weeks.

Source: The Jerusalem Post, Al Jazeera

Canada and US race to strike trade deal before August 19 tariff deadline

Canadian and U.S. officials held daily negotiations in Washington this week to avert new 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, which President Trump has said will take effect August 19 on roughly $20 billion of imports with no carve-outs under the USMCA. Talks reportedly cover scrapping Canada's retaliatory auto tariffs, easing provincial restrictions on U.S. liquor sales and adjusting dairy quotas. A Canadian government source said Thursday that Washington also wants a deal before the deadline, though Ottawa was unsatisfied with a U.S. proposal presented earlier in the week.

Source: U.S. News & World Report (Reuters)

Sudan's RSF launches large drone barrage on Khartoum, advances near Ethiopian border

The paramilitary Rapid Support Forces struck Khartoum and several army-held cities, including El-Obeid, Atbara and El-Damer, with drones on August 12 in what appeared to be its largest coordinated attack in months. Separately, RSF and allied Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North fighters advanced into Kurmuk, a town in Blue Nile State on the border with Ethiopia, as army forces withdrew, after the two sides earlier claimed to have seized the nearby town of Geissan. The escalation comes as the RSF also faces a string of defections, including a regional governor who resigned and left the group.

Source: Al Jazeera, Asharq Al-Awsat
