Saudi Arabia shuts key oil pipeline after drone attack; Trump says Iran "probably" responsible

Saudi Arabia temporarily shut down its 1,200-kilometer East-West pipeline, which carries 4-5 million barrels of crude a day and lets the kingdom bypass the blockaded Strait of Hormuz, after drones launched from Iraq struck it near Medina and sparked a fire. No group claimed responsibility, but President Trump told reporters on Saturday he believed Iran was "probably" behind the strike. The attack compounds pressure on Saudi oil exports already squeezed by the Hormuz closure and comes as Iraqi authorities investigate drone launch sites found near the Iran-Iraq border.

Source: ABC News, The Times of Israel

Houthis cement control of Yemen's Red Sea coast and Bab el-Mandeb Strait

After a week-long offensive, Iran-backed Houthi forces captured Yemen's Red Sea coast and three strategic islands, including Mayyun Island in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, through which roughly 12% of world shipping passes. It is the group's largest territorial gain since a 2022 truce and follows its capture of the port of Mokha days earlier. Trump said the Houthis had told his administration they did not want the US directly involved in the fighting, and Washington is offering Saudi Arabia intelligence and targeting support rather than launching its own strikes for now.

Source: NPR, Al Jazeera

Putin warns Europe against sending troops to Ukraine as BRICS summit opens in New Delhi

Speaking to reporters on the sidelines of the 18th BRICS summit in New Delhi, President Putin said any deployment of European troops to Ukraine would amount to direct conflict with Russia, while insisting Moscow does not intend to threaten European countries. The warning came as the summit, attended by Xi Jinping, Narendra Modi and other leaders of the 11-nation bloc, opened with Modi telling delegates the "Global South is in the front row of global crises, but in the back row of decision-making." Xi separately called on BRICS members to help end the Middle East war, saying it serves no country's interests.

Source: Washington Times, ThePrint

Russian drone kills two in Zaporizhzhia as Moscow keeps up nationwide strikes

A Russian drone destroyed a private home in Zaporizhzhia overnight, killing a 64-year-old woman and a 37-year-old man and trapping others under rubble, part of a broader barrage that Ukrainian officials said included roughly 1,000 strikes against 45 settlements in the region over 24 hours. The attack came the same day Putin warned Europe against deploying troops to Ukraine, and follows a week in which Russian strikes have increasingly targeted urban fuel stations and food infrastructure alongside the power grid ahead of winter.

Source: Kyiv Post, Ukrinform

UN extends partial Sudan arms embargo 30 days as US pushes for nationwide ban

The UN Security Council voted unanimously to extend the existing arms embargo on Sudan's Darfur region for another month, giving members more time to negotiate a US-backed proposal to expand it to cover the entire country and pressure the warring army and Rapid Support Forces to end their more than three-year war. Russia, which holds a Security Council veto and has opposed a broader embargo, said through its deputy UN ambassador that it "will not allow even a hint of broadening the restrictions." The war has displaced roughly 11.8 million people and left millions facing acute hunger or famine.

Source: The Washington Post (AP), The National
