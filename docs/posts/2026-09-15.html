Iran's security chief rejects talks with US despite Trump's openness to negotiate

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, rejected any resumption of talks with the United States on Tuesday after President Trump signaled he was open to renewed negotiations over the Middle East war. "Don't get distracted by the US president's mixed signals — from 'no negotiations' to 'we're ready to talk,'" Rezaei wrote on X, adding "No talks until Iran's conditions are met. Period!" Iran has listed war reparations and the lifting of sanctions among its preconditions for ending the conflict and its blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

Source: Business Recorder, The Times of Israel

Houthi attacks wound 13 in three Saudi cities as Mecca briefly issues security alert

A Saudi-led coalition said 13 people were injured after Yemen's Houthi rebels struck three Saudi cities on Monday, the latest in a wave of attacks that has hit the kingdom since the group broke a years-long truce. Saudi civil defense briefly issued an alert warning of possible danger in Mecca, home to Islam's holiest site — the first such warning there during the current escalation — before saying minutes later that the danger had cleared. A Houthi official separately said Saudi airstrikes in Yemen had killed three people.

Source: Haaretz, AP via Click2Houston

China warns it will cancel Sept. 24 Trump-Xi summit over Taiwan arms sale

Beijing's foreign ministry delivered a formal warning that it would call off Xi Jinping's planned September 24 White House visit — the two leaders' third face-to-face meeting in a year — if Washington approves a pending $14 billion arms package for Taiwan, which China calls a "red line" in bilateral relations. Trump said Sunday he was "not worried" Xi would cancel, telling reporters "we have a great relationship," even as he continues weighing whether to approve the sale ahead of the summit.

Source: Taipei Times, The Standard

Sweden's parties launch coalition talks after knife-edge election leaves no clear winner

Sweden's political blocs began coalition negotiations Monday after Sunday's election produced a razor-thin, still-uncertain result, with opposition parties led by Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson narrowly ahead of Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's governing alliance, 176 seats to 173 of 349. Both Andersson and Kristersson said they would explore paths to power, though analysts warn the process could be prolonged: the Centre Party has ruled out joining a government reliant on the Sweden Democrats, while the Liberals and Christian Democrats have ruled out working with the Social Democrats. Final results, including overseas ballots, were due Wednesday.

Source: AP via Washington Times

Israeli strikes kill Palestinians in Gaza as ceasefire tensions persist

At least two Palestinians were killed and more than 20 injured in Israeli strikes across Gaza on Monday, including one that hit a tent sheltering displaced people in the al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis, as Israel continued near-daily strikes despite the US-backed ceasefire that took effect in October 2025. The strikes come days after US envoy Jared Kushner's visit to the region and cast fresh doubt on the durability of the truce, even as Hamas has agreed in principle to a disarmament roadmap.

Source: PBS NewsHour

UN warns foreign networks are fueling Sudan's war as RSF siege of El Obeid tightens

The United Nations' human rights chief said the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces are carrying out "relentless" drone attacks on El Obeid, the besieged capital of North Kordofan state, warning that foreign recruitment and external support networks — including reported involvement of foreign mercenaries — continue to fuel Sudan's civil war. About 500,000 people, including 105,000 already displaced, remain trapped in the city, which sits on a key route linking RSF-held Darfur to army-controlled central Sudan, raising fears it could suffer a massacre similar to El Fasher's.

Source: UN News, Al Jazeera
