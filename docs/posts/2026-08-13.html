August 13, 2026

US-Israel conflict with Iran continues amid Strait of Hormuz talks

Fighting linked to the ongoing US-Israel war on Iran continued this week even as diplomatic efforts advanced. Mediators, including Oman and Pakistan, have been pushing talks aimed at reopening the Strait of Hormuz, with Tehran and Washington reported to be nearing a deal on the blockade. Israeli strikes on Gaza and Lebanon have continued in parallel, and U.S. officials have said they are seeking compensation from Iran alongside the negotiations.

Ukraine strikes Russian Black Sea Fleet port at Novorossiysk

Ukraine carried out a drone strike on Russia's Black Sea Fleet facilities at Novorossiysk, with explosions also reported near Sevastopol and the Crimean Bridge in occupied Crimea. Ukrainian officials said several Russian warships were damaged. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia does not want to end the war but that Ukraine "will stop it," while fighting elsewhere along the front continued to produce casualties on both sides.

Gaza ceasefire remains fragile despite new disarmament roadmap

A ceasefire that took effect in October 2025 remains in place but fragile. Mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the United States recently finalized a roadmap for the next phase, under which Hamas would disarm in stages while Israel withdraws forces over time. Israel has not formally accepted the latest proposal, and casualties have continued to be reported in Gaza despite the truce.

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey sign Mecca Joint Defense Agreement

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey signed a trilateral mutual defense pact in Mecca on August 7, building on an earlier Saudi-Pakistan defense agreement from September 2025. The pact states that an armed attack on any one of the three states will be treated as an attack on all three. Turkish officials have said they hope to expand the arrangement to include additional countries in the region.

Taiwan steps up defense preparations as China holds drills

Tensions across the Taiwan Strait remain elevated, with Beijing conducting military exercises that Washington has criticized as unnecessarily escalatory. Taiwan's legislature has approved roughly $25 billion in additional defense spending through 2033, and the island is testing measures such as relocating weapons production and limiting mobile network speeds during civil defense drills meant to simulate wartime conditions.

South China Sea tensions test regional middle powers

Territorial disputes in the South China Sea, including around Indonesia's Natuna Sea, continue to strain relations between China and its neighbors. Indonesia is seeking to balance protection of its maritime sovereignty with its deepening economic ties to China, in what analysts describe as a broader test of whether smaller and mid-sized states can rely on international law to safeguard their interests in the region.
