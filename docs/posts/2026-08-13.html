Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan sign mutual defense pact

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed the "Mecca Joint Deterrence Agreement" on August 7, a tripartite pact stipulating that an armed attack on any signatory will be treated as an attack on all three. The agreement, built on an earlier Saudi-Pakistan defense deal, follows nearly a year of negotiations and is described by officials as defensive rather than aimed at a specific adversary, though it comes amid heightened regional tension over Iran and Israel. The pact is open to other regional states wishing to join.

Source: Al Jazeera

Iraq warns Iran-backed militias of terrorism charges over disarmament deadline

Iraq's top political leadership issued its sharpest warning yet to Iran-aligned armed factions, saying groups that fail to disarm by a September 30 deadline will face terrorism charges. The deadline coincides with the date set for the withdrawal of the US-led coalition under a 2024 Baghdad-Washington agreement. Only a handful of militias have so far agreed to integrate into Iraq's official security forces, while cross-border attacks on US and Gulf targets by pro-Tehran factions have drawn retaliatory strikes that have reportedly killed at least 20 fighters.

Source: The National

US-Iran talks remain stalled as Washington pivots back to sanctions

Negotiations between the United States and Iran remain deadlocked, with Tehran demanding the release of roughly $12 billion in assets frozen in Qatar as a precondition for further talks, following a setback in a recent round of discussions in Doha. With diplomatic momentum stalling, the Trump administration has shifted back toward sanctions pressure as its primary lever, even as officials express hope that economic pressure can still force a breakthrough.

Source: NPR

Putin threatens to seize European ships over "shadow fleet" crackdown

Russian President Vladimir Putin said on August 12 that Moscow would seize ships belonging to European countries if they moved to sell cargo confiscated from vessels suspected of belonging to Russia's sanctions-evading "shadow fleet," calling European seizure efforts "nothing less than piracy and robbery." The warning, delivered during Pacific Fleet exercises, follows the EU's latest sanctions package targeting Russia's oil trade; by late June nine suspected shadow-fleet tankers had already been seized across Europe, including a British interception in the English Channel.

Source: RFE/RL, Euronews

US ambassador calls West Bank settler siege a "horrific act of terror"

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee condemned as a "horrific act of terror" an ongoing siege by Jewish settlers of a Palestinian-American family's home in the village of Qusra in the Israeli-occupied West Bank. Since Sunday, a group of settlers has gathered near the home, thrown stones, attempted to breach a wall and prevented occupants from leaving despite the Israeli military's presence, according to family members, who say two relatives inside have gone days without food or water. The rebuke was notably sharp for a US official, even as Huckabee defended the Israeli military's handling of the incident.

Source: NPR

Moscow signals readiness to resume Ukraine talks on Istanbul framework

Russia said it remains ready to resume peace negotiations with Ukraine on the basis of proposals discussed in earlier Istanbul talks, with President Putin citing "the agreements reached in Istanbul, on the modalities discussed in Anchorage, and, most importantly, the realities on the ground" as the basis for any resumption. Kyiv has said it is open to talks but insists any settlement must preserve Ukraine's territorial integrity, leaving the two sides' conditions for restarting negotiations still far apart as fighting and drone strikes continue along the front line.

Source: Daily Sabah, Kyiv Post
