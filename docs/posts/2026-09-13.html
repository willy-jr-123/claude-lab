Houthis capture Bab el-Mandeb island as Trump shrugs off Gulf-Iran Hormuz talks

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels captured Perim Island at the mouth of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a day after seizing the Red Sea port of Mokha, tightening their grip on a waterway that now carries roughly 8.1 million barrels of oil a day as Saudi Arabia has increasingly rerouted exports there to avoid the blockaded Strait of Hormuz. Asked about a planned Monday meeting in Oman where Iran, Iraq and Gulf Arab states are due to discuss safe shipping through Hormuz, President Trump told reporters at his golf course in Ireland he "doesn't care," saying "that's up to them, that's fine, they could be happy."

Source: NPR, CNBC

China threatens to cancel Sept. 24 Trump-Xi summit over Taiwan arms sale

Beijing has warned Washington it will scrap the planned September 24 White House meeting between Xi Jinping and President Trump, their third face-to-face encounter in a year, if the administration approves a $14 billion arms package for Taiwan that Congress authorized in January. Foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun called China's opposition "consistent, clear-cut and resolute" and described Taiwan as a red line in the relationship. Trump has been counting on the summit to boost his standing ahead of November's midterm elections, making a cancellation politically costly for him.

Source: Yahoo News, Kyiv Post

Russia hits Ukrainian power sites as Kyiv strikes Russian oil-refining hub

Russia struck energy facilities and other targets across southern Ukraine overnight, part of a barrage that included 129 attack drones, eight Kalibr cruise missiles and Iskander-M/KN-23 ballistic missiles, while Ukrainian forces hit an oil-refining hub deep inside Russian territory. The exchange came as debate continued over when and where new peace talks might resume, following weekend visits to Moscow and Kyiv by US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner that produced no breakthrough.

Source: Bloomberg

Algeria severs diplomatic ties with UAE over alleged destabilization campaign

Algeria cut diplomatic relations with the United Arab Emirates and moved to close its airspace to Emirati traffic, with its foreign ministry saying the step came "after exhausting all avenues to preserve bilateral relations." Algiers accused Abu Dhabi of "provocative and hostile acts" and of working to destabilize Libya, Sudan and Mali, though commercial flights to and from Algiers will continue until the end of the year. The rupture marks one of the most serious diplomatic breaks between Arab states in recent years.

Source: Al Jazeera

Iran and UAE seek to "turn the page" as Pezeshkian meets Abu Dhabi crown prince

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian held talks with Abu Dhabi Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in New Delhi, their first known face-to-face meeting since the West Asia war began in February. Pezeshkian said the two agreed to "look to the future and build it together," a notable thaw after Iran struck US bases in the UAE earlier in the conflict and relations between Tehran and Abu Dhabi deteriorated sharply.

Source: Business Standard

Sweden votes in knife-edge election that could bring far right into government

Swedes went to the polls in a general election too close to call between Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's four-party centre-right alliance, which includes the nationalist Sweden Democrats, and a four-party left-wing opposition bloc. Polls had long shown the left with a comfortable lead, but the race narrowed to a dead heat in the campaign's final days, raising the prospect that the Sweden Democrats could enter government for the first time.
