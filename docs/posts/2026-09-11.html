Houthi rebels seize Yemen's Mokha port and a Red Sea island near Bab el-Mandeb

Iran-backed Houthi forces captured the port city of Mokha on Yemen's Red Sea coast after a rapid offensive that pushed out Saudi-backed government forces, and also took a strategic island near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, roughly 80 kilometers away, through which about 12% of world shipping typically passes. It is the Houthis' largest territorial gain since a 2022 truce paused Yemen's civil war, and the surge in fighting over the past week has displaced more than 46,000 people, raising fears the truce has effectively collapsed and that Yemen is sliding back into all-out war.

Source: NPR, The National

US Treasury sanctions Hezbollah and Iranian-proxy financial networks across four countries

The Treasury Department imposed a new round of sanctions targeting exchange houses, gold traders and individuals in Iraq, Lebanon, the UAE and Turkey accused of moving more than $1 billion from Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps to Hezbollah and Iraq's Kata'ib Hezbollah since January 2025. The move is the latest step in "Operation Economic Outcast," launched August 24 to choke off funding for Iran's war effort, missile program, cyber operations and the IRGC. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the campaign would "find, cut off, and dismantle" networks keeping the Iranian regime afloat financially.

Source: The Jerusalem Post

Russian strikes kill at least five in Pavlohrad and hit a Kyiv petrol station

Russian drones struck the southeastern industrial city of Pavlohrad, killing at least five people, while a separate strike hit a Ukrnafta petrol station in Kyiv, injuring several people according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko — reportedly the first jet-powered drone attack on a fuel station in the capital. A Bunge-operated agribusiness facility in nearby Dnipro was also hit. The attacks add to an intensifying Russian air campaign in recent weeks that has increasingly targeted urban fuel stations and food-distribution sites, which officials say is aimed at disrupting civilian life and morale.

Source: Cyprus Mail (AFP), Washington Times

Putin arrives in New Delhi for BRICS summit, holds talks with Modi

Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in New Delhi for a three-day visit centered on the 18th BRICS Summit, holding bilateral talks with Prime Minister Narendra Modi expected to focus on energy, defense and trade ties, including civil nuclear cooperation, fertilizers and cross-border payments in domestic currencies. It is the leaders' second meeting in two weeks, following talks on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek on August 31, where Modi urged Putin to work toward ending the war in Ukraine.

Source: Dawn

Gulf states weigh first talks with Iran on Strait of Hormuz since war began

Oman is working to convene foreign ministers from the six-member Gulf Cooperation Council and Iran in the southern city of Salalah on Monday, in what would be the first such gathering since the US and Israel began bombing Iran more than six months ago and effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz to shipping. The meeting is not yet confirmed, and escalating fighting between Saudi-backed forces and Houthi rebels in Yemen could complicate the plans. Washington has maintained a naval blockade meant to pressure Tehran into allowing free passage through the strait, which Iran says it won't discuss until the blockade is lifted.

Source: Bloomberg

Hong Kong court jails Tiananmen vigil organizers for up to seven years

A Hong Kong court sentenced three former leaders of the disbanded Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China to lengthy prison terms for inciting subversion under the national security law. Chow Hang-tung received seven years and three months, Lee Cheuk-yan seven years, and Albert Ho, 74, five years and two months after pleading guilty. Judges cited the group's use of the slogan "end one-party dictatorship" in connection with the annual vigils it organized to mark Beijing's 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown, in a case widely seen as a marker of the city's shrinking political freedoms.

Source: Al Jazeera, CNN
