September 2, 2026

US launches fresh strikes on Iranian targets near Strait of Hormuz as Iran hits bases across the region

US Central Command struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps military and communication sites in Iran on Tuesday, reigniting a six-month-old conflict that has already driven global energy prices higher. Iran responded with missile and drone strikes on US-linked assets in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Iraq, and claimed to have killed a large number of US forces in Jordan, though US officials said there were no casualties. The exchange followed a weekend clash and Monday's mining of two tankers in the strait; Brent crude jumped roughly 4% on the news as Tehran vowed to keep blocking oil exports from the Gulf.

Source: Military Times, CBS News

Russian drones hit Kyiv university and homes on seventh straight day of strikes

Russian Shahed-type drones struck a university in central Kyiv during classes and damaged a six-story residential building in the Shevchenkivskyi district on Wednesday, part of a barrage that injured at least seven people and hit a medical facility, a kindergarten and several other buildings. Ukraine's air force said it shot down 141 of the 174 drones and missiles launched overnight. It was the seventh consecutive day Russia has struck the Ukrainian capital, and the attacks also caused power outages in five regions, with Odesa hit hardest.

Source: Kyiv Post, Cyprus Mail

Israel says it captured Hamas internal security chief in Gaza City raid

Israeli special forces captured Muein al-Arabid, identified as the head of Hamas's internal security apparatus, during a raid in western Gaza City, Defense Minister Israel Katz said Tuesday. Separately, Katz said Israel remains ready to facilitate the emigration of Palestinians from Gaza "by any means" but that the plan has been "frozen" by President Trump. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu visited an Israeli military post inside Gaza the same day, as sporadic strikes continued despite the US-backed ceasefire in place since October.

Source: The Times of Israel, The National

UN halves food aid to West Bank as funding shortfall deepens

The UN World Food Programme said Tuesday it is cutting the number of people it supports in the occupied West Bank from 400,000 to 200,000 because of critical funding shortages, and warned that further cuts may be unavoidable. The agency said it urgently needs an additional $386 million over the next six months to serve roughly two million food-insecure people across Gaza and the West Bank, where movement restrictions, demolitions and displacement have sharply worsened food insecurity since 2023.

Source: World Food Programme, NBC News

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll tops 1,100 as thousands remain missing

The death toll from last month's Himalayan glacial-lake outburst flood rose to 1,114 in Nepal, with roughly 3,916 people still listed as missing there, while Chinese officials reported 16 dead and 546 missing on the Tibetan side. The US State Department said it was still tracking about 75 unaccounted-for Americans in the cross-border region. Rescue teams continue searching in Nepal's northern Rasuwa district, where a glacial collapse on August 26 sent rock, ice and meltwater surging into the Trishuli River valley.

Source: ABC News

UN says world will miss 1.5°C climate target, warns of "unavoidable" overshoot

A UN Environment Programme report released Wednesday concluded that global warming will exceed the 1.5°C threshold set by the Paris Agreement, likely within the next few years, calling the overshoot "unavoidable" and some resulting impacts "irreversible." Under the most optimistic scenario, with aggressive climate action, warming would peak near 1.8°C around mid-century before declining; under current government policies, the report projects warming of about 2.6°C by 2100. UNEP said the focus must now shift from preventing the overshoot to limiting its size and duration and steering temperatures back down.

Source: UNEP, NBC News
