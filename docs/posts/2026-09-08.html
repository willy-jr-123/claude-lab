Russia resumes missile and drone barrage on Kyiv as 72-hour pause for Witkoff-Kushner visit expires

Russian forces struck Kyiv with ballistic and cruise missiles, anti-ship missiles and more than 160 attack drones early Tuesday, killing at least two people and wounding ten others, hours after a 72-hour truce covering the capital lapsed following US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner's separate talks with Presidents Zelensky and Putin. The strikes sparked large fires across the city and came despite Witkoff's statement that "substantive progress" had been made toward scheduling further trilateral talks, underscoring the gap between diplomatic signals and continued fighting on the ground.

Source: CBS News, Euronews

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia wound more than 70 in major escalation of Yemen war

Yemen's Houthi rebels launched a wave of drone and missile strikes on the Saudi cities of Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait on Tuesday, igniting fires at oil facilities and other infrastructure and wounding more than 70 people, in one of the most significant attacks on the kingdom in years. The Houthis said the strikes were retaliation for a Saudi-backed airstrike on a prison in Yemen's Jawf province that killed at least seven people, marking a sharp widening of the conflict after clashes between the rebels and Saudi-backed government forces shattered a years-long truce in Yemen's civil war.

Source: The Washington Post, CNN

UK, France and Canada ban trade with Israeli West Bank settlements, drawing US and Israeli rebuke

Britain announced it will ban imports of goods produced in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, along with related financing, construction and real estate services, with Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband accusing settlers of conducting what UK officials termed "ethnic cleansing." France and Canada moved to join the ban the same day. US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee called the move discriminatory against Jewish people, while Israeli President Isaac Herzog accused Britain of interfering in Israel's upcoming election and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for the British ambassador in Tel Aviv to be expelled.

Source: NBC News, Al Jazeera

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20 billion of US goods take effect as trade war deepens

Canada's counter-tariffs on American products took effect just after midnight Tuesday, covering an estimated $20 billion in US goods including steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment and electronics, with duties ranging from 15% to 50%. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada would "match Washington's new tariffs dollar for dollar" after trade talks between the two countries collapsed last month, extending a standoff that has strained what was long one of the world's largest bilateral trading relationships.

Source: The Washington Post, NBC News

Serbia's government moves to dissolve parliament, setting up snap election as Vucic eyes PM role

Serbian Prime Minister Djuro Macut called on the government to dissolve parliament, opening the path to a snap election that President Aleksandar Vucic has said could be held on October 18 or 25, more than a year ahead of schedule. The move follows nearly two years of sustained anti-corruption protests triggered by a 2024 railway station canopy collapse that killed 16 people, and is widely seen as a bid by Vucic to reassert control, with speculation he may seek to become prime minister himself.

Source: Bloomberg, Al Jazeera

Nigeria reportedly struck secret ceasefire with Boko Haram after mass kidnapping

Nigeria's government has quietly maintained a ceasefire with Boko Haram for about three months, sources told The New Humanitarian, after negotiations secured the release in June of 360 civilians abducted from Ngoshe village in Borno State during a March attack on a nearby army base. The truce, reportedly likely to be extended by another 40 days, marks a possible shift toward talks after 17 years of conflict, even as the government publicly denies negotiating with the jihadist group and analysts caution it may be only a tactical pause rather than a path to lasting peace.

Source: The New Humanitarian, Premium Times
