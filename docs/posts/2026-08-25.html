Tanker disabled by strike in Strait of Hormuz as US launches "Operation Economic Outcast" against Iran

An oil tanker was struck by an unknown projectile a few miles off the coast of Oman late Monday, disabling its engine, Britain's UK Maritime Trade Organization reported; the crew was unharmed and there were no immediate signs of an oil spill. The attack came as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent detailed a new sanctions campaign, dubbed "Operation Economic Outcast," targeting Iran's oil exports, shipping, aviation, gold and digital-asset networks, plus secondary sanctions on countries and firms that keep doing business with Tehran. Iran's security chief vowed to "neutralize" the economic pressure, even as a Pakistani delegation wrapped up mediation talks in Tehran aimed at ending the nearly six-month-old war.

Source: U.S. News (AP), CNBC

Canada unveils retaliatory tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods as trade war deepens

Canada announced countertariffs of up to 50% on roughly $27.6 billion worth of American goods — including steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and paper, seafood, furniture and motorcycles — set to take effect September 8. Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said the measures match US duties "dollar for dollar, rate for rate," and Ottawa also pledged $7.5 billion in support for Canadian workers and businesses hurt by the dispute. The move follows Washington's 50% tariffs on Canadian goods and autos after trade talks broke down last week, with Prime Minister Mark Carney saying Canada had "got attacked."

Source: CNBC, The Hill

Israeli strikes kill four, including two children, a week after US pressed for restraint in Gaza

Israeli strikes and tank fire killed at least four Palestinians in Gaza, including two children, hospital officials said. A strike on a building in the Bureij refugee camp in central Gaza, following an evacuation warning, killed a 4-year-old and wounded his mother and sister; separately, Israeli tank fire killed a 10-year-old girl in Khan Younis, an incident the Israeli military said it was not aware of. The deaths came roughly a week after a US delegation reportedly asked Israel to draw down attacks as part of the fragile Gaza ceasefire and disarmament roadmap.

Source: Washington Post (AP)

Putin signs decree letting Russia seize poorly protected infrastructure as Ukraine's drone campaign persists

President Vladimir Putin signed a decree allowing the Russian state to take temporary control of critical infrastructure whose owners fail to adequately protect or repair it after attacks, a move that follows a sustained Ukrainian drone campaign against Russian refineries and energy sites that has triggered fuel shortages in parts of Russia. Ukraine reported continuing heavy Russian losses in matériel and troops, while Kyiv keeps up long-range strikes, including on retail and logistics infrastructure such as the Wildberries online retailer, as both sides trade near-daily attacks with no ceasefire in sight.

Source: Al Jazeera

Taiwan indicts nine over illegal AI server exports to China involving Nvidia and Super Micro staff

Taiwanese prosecutors indicted nine people, including an Nvidia Taiwan employee and two Super Micro Taiwan employees, for allegedly smuggling high-end AI servers to Chinese customers in violation of export controls. Of 130 Nvidia B300-based servers ordered, prosecutors said 74 were shipped to China via direct routes and transshipment through Indonesia, Japan and Hong Kong, using forged documents claiming the equipment would stay in Taiwan; 56 more servers were seized at the border. Prosecutors are seeking up to five years in prison for seven defendants, underscoring the widening front of the US-China tech rivalry that runs through Taiwan's chip supply chain.

Source: Washington Post (AP), Al Jazeera

RSF raids displace hundreds more near El Obeid despite battlefield setbacks and defections

Rapid Support Forces fighters raided the villages of Um Dareisa and Al-Shaqla southwest of El Obeid in Sudan's North Kordofan state, killing civilians and prompting more than 1,000 people from 215 families to flee on foot to the state capital, arriving in critical condition after walking dozens of kilometers; local officials accused RSF fighters of unlawful killings, assaults and sexual violence. The attacks came even as the RSF faces mounting battlefield setbacks in Darfur and a fresh wave of defections, including a regional governor and a senior field commander who left with roughly 300 fighters, part of a broader unraveling of paramilitary control in central Sudan.

Source: Sudan Tribune
