Putin says chance remains for Ukraine peace deal as Kyiv cites "new dynamic"

Speaking at an economic forum in Russia's Far East, President Vladimir Putin said a chance remains to reach an agreement ending the war in Ukraine, adding that the United States and China are among countries ready to support a settlement. He said Ukrainian attacks on civilian merchant ships and warnings about strikes on civilian aircraft amount to "acts of state terrorism" that complicate prospects for talks, though he said contacts with Kyiv continue. No formal peace talks have been held since February even as the war grinds into its fifth year. Ukrainian officials said they see a "new dynamic" in the search for a settlement, though fighting continues on both sides.

Source: The Japan Times, RTÉ

Netanyahu says toppling Iran's regime is Israel's "central mission," calls goal "within reach"

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told the IDF General Staff Forum during a Rosh Hashanah toast that regime change in Iran is now Israel's "central mission," describing Tehran's government as "faltering" and "fighting for its survival." He credited Israeli military operations since the war with Iran began earlier this year with weakening the regime, and said he was confident in Israel's "ability to remove this threat once and for all." The remarks are among the most explicit yet from Netanyahu naming regime change as a stated Israeli war aim.

Source: The Times of Israel, JNS

Vance rules out renewed Iran talks until Tehran halts Strait of Hormuz shipping attacks

Vice President JD Vance said the United States is maintaining a "silent treatment" toward Iran and will not resume diplomatic talks until Iranian forces stop targeting commercial vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Washington has not held talks with Tehran since the two sides signed a Pakistan-brokered memorandum of understanding in June, an arrangement that collapsed in July when Iranian forces resumed attacks on shipping. US-coordinated escort operations through Omani waters continue to move oil out of the Gulf, though volumes remain far below prewar levels as tankers often transit at night to avoid detection.

Source: The Washington Times

Israeli strikes kill three in Gaza as far-right minister unveils mass expulsion plan

Israeli forces killed at least three Palestinians in Gaza on Thursday despite the US-brokered ceasefire, the latest in a string of incidents that Palestinian officials and rights groups say violate the truce. Separately, far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir outlined a proposal for a future Israeli government to establish a dedicated ministry to oversee the mass emigration of Palestinians from Gaza. The developments came as Amnesty International accused Israel of accelerating land confiscation in the occupied West Bank as part of what the group called an "annexation agenda."

Source: Democracy Now!

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on US goods set to take effect September 8

Prime Minister Mark Carney's government is set to impose tariffs ranging from 15% to 50% on more than 700 categories of US goods starting September 8, doubling duties on US steel and aluminum to 50% and adding levies on dairy and electronics. The move follows Washington's 50% tariff on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods and marks a further escalation of the trade war, with Carney saying Canada will "match Washington's new tariffs dollar for dollar" to protect Canadian workers and businesses.

Source: Al Jazeera

India to host expanded BRICS summit in New Delhi amid global trade turmoil

India will host the 18th BRICS Summit on September 12-13 in New Delhi, bringing together the expanded 11-member bloc — Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Indonesia — under the theme "Building Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability." Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are both expected to attend in person, making it one of India's most closely watched diplomatic events in years as the grouping seeks to present a united front amid tariff battles and supply-chain disruptions involving Washington.

Source: Foreign Policy, Eurasia Business News
