Kushner meets Hamas leadership in Egypt as Gaza roadmap push continues

White House envoy Jared Kushner held a rare, direct meeting with Hamas political chief Khalil al-Hayya and other Hamas officials in Egypt, in a fresh attempt to salvage the stalled 15-point Gaza ceasefire roadmap. The session, described by a source close to the talks as productive, was Kushner's first direct meeting with Hamas leadership since last October's ceasefire agreement. Kushner was due to travel on to Israel on Monday to press Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who has told the envoy that Israel will not withdraw forces from Gaza or allow reconstruction until Hamas fully disarms.

Source: Haaretz

US-Iran 60-day deadline lapses as Tehran shifts to "fully offensive" posture

A 60-day deadline for the US and Iran to reach a deal ending their conflict, set under a memorandum of understanding agreed in June, passed with both sides essentially as far apart as when it was signed. Iranian hardliners have reportedly been preparing for a wider conflict rather than banking on the MOU, giving the Revolutionary Guard Corps more control over the army and ramping up missile and drone production, while Tehran has set a new deadline of several weeks for Washington to fully implement the memorandum. Separately, Iran is working to finalize a joint statement with Oman on managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, though vessel traffic through the strait fell nearly 20% last week amid continuing disruption.

Source: The Washington Post, CNN

Russia and Ukraine trade strikes as Kyiv braces for more drone and missile attacks

Fighting continued along the front and in rear-area strikes, with Ukrainian border guards hitting Russian troop shelters in the Southern Slobozhanshchyna sector and Ukrainian forces reporting damage to several logistics centers used by Russian marketplace Wildberries. Robert "Magyar" Brovdi, commander of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces, warned that Russia is set to intensify missile and jet-powered drone strikes in the coming weeks. Russia's Defense Ministry put its cumulative personnel losses since the February 2022 invasion at roughly 1.47 million, a figure Kyiv uses to argue Moscow's offensive is unsustainable, as the war approaches its fifth year with no ceasefire in sight.

Source: Ukrinform

Canada and US down to two days before 50% tariffs take effect

Canadian and US officials met again on Sunday, with Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer holding an hour-long virtual session described as "constructive," but no deal was reached with just two days left before Washington's threatened 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods are due to take effect on August 19. LeBlanc and Canada's chief negotiator Janice Charette spent the weekend in Washington continuing talks. Conservative opposition leader Pierre Poilievre said he saw "no reason" Prime Minister Mark Carney could not secure a deal before the deadline, while a former Canadian diplomat warned the standoff "looks like it's going to end in tears."

Source: BNN Bloomberg, CTV News

Fallout continues from Turkey-Saudi-Pakistan "Mecca" defense pact

Regional reaction continued to the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement signed on August 7 in Mecca by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, under which an attack on any signatory would be treated as an attack on all. Analysts describe the pact — combining roughly 1.4 million active troops, 3,400 aircraft and more than 340 naval assets across the three countries — as a framework for closer strategic and military coordination rather than a NATO-style alliance, but note it marks a significant shift toward a more multipolar security order in the Middle East and South Asia. The agreement follows months of regional turmoil, including Israel's expanded military reach and the ongoing US-Iran confrontation.

Source: Atlantic Council, Al Jazeera

China's presence near Thitu Island keeps South China Sea tensions elevated

Chinese Coast Guard and navy vessels remained active near Thitu Island (Pag-asa), the Philippines' main outpost in the Spratly Islands, with Chinese helicopters conducting reconnaissance flights over the area as Manila pushes ahead with infrastructure upgrades there and on nearby Nanshan Island. The United States has stepped up joint activity with the Philippines, including over 500 planned exercises in 2026 and joint patrols with Australia under the Maritime Cooperative Activity framework, aimed at countering Chinese maneuvers within Manila's exclusive economic zone. Analysts continue to flag the South China Sea, through which roughly a fifth of global trade passes, as one of the region's most dangerous potential flashpoints.

Source: ABC News
