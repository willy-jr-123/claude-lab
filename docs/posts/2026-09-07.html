Zelensky says war likely to continue into winter as US peace push yields no breakthrough

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Sunday that Ukraine's war with Russia will probably continue into winter, after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner wrapped up talks in Kyiv without announcing a breakthrough, a day after meeting President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. Zelensky said he hoped for an agreement with US partners and was counting on European support "if the war continues in winter — and this is how it appears at the moment," while Witkoff and Kushner said they wanted to work toward a "refreshed" peace proposal. President Trump was expected to be briefed by his envoys before speaking with Putin and then Zelensky.

Source: The Kyiv Independent, Euronews

Israeli airstrikes on southern Lebanon village kill at least 11, including children

Israel's air force carried out strikes early Monday on the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Rumman, killing at least 11 people, including two children and four women, and wounding more than a dozen others, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry. A separate Israeli drone strike hit a civilian car, killing two medics inside. The Israeli military said it had targeted Hezbollah operatives moving weapons into the town and was investigating reports of civilian casualties, as the attack marked a sharp escalation despite a truce reached between Israel and Hezbollah more than two months ago.

Source: Al Jazeera, NBC News

Israeli strikes kill five Palestinians in Gaza, including two children, despite ceasefire

Israeli attacks in Gaza killed five Palestinians on Sunday, including two children, according to Palestinian officials, despite the ceasefire nominally in place in the territory. Among the dead were seven-year-old Wafaa Aqila and her father, killed when an Israeli strike hit the vehicle they were traveling in after buying school supplies, days before she was due to start the new school year. Separately, the Israeli military said it killed Yusuf Aqila, a company commander in Hamas's elite Nukhba Force, saying he had been advancing attacks on troops and civilians and attempting to rebuild Hamas capabilities "in a systematic violation of the ceasefire."

Source: Al Jazeera

Iran plans Hormuz "exclusion zone" as tanker war with US escalates and oil prices spike

The head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council said Monday that Tehran plans to announce a maritime "exclusion zone" outside the Strait of Hormuz where it intends to stop vessels transiting the waterway without Iranian permission, even as Iranian officials said a deal with Oman on a temporary safe shipping route through the strait was in its final stages. The announcement follows a sharp escalation over the past 24 hours in which US forces disabled or destroyed three Iranian oil tankers and Iran fired on US warships and struck tankers of its own, sending Brent crude briefly to a near seven-week high of about $97 a barrel. Iran's parliament speaker warned of strikes on US energy firms in the region after Washington threatened to "destroy" Iranian tankers.

Source: Al Jazeera, Bloomberg

Germany's far-right AfD wins historic state election, falls short of governing majority

The Alternative for Germany (AfD) won 43.8% of the vote in Sunday's Saxony-Anhalt state election, its strongest result in any German state election and enough to put the far-right party on the cusp of power at the state level for the first time since World War Two. The result gave the AfD 39 of 83 seats, just short of the 42 needed for an outright majority, though it could reach a majority with the small anti-immigration BSW party, which took 5.3% of the vote. Chancellor Friedrich Merz's conservatives trailed at 18.3%, with the Social Democrats and Greens also clearing the threshold to enter parliament.

Source: CNN, NPR

North Korea commissions second nuclear-capable destroyer as US, South Korea and Japan hold drills

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un commissioned the destroyer Kang Kon, his navy's second 5,000-ton warship capable of carrying nuclear and conventional weapons, at a ceremony in Wonsan, and called for building a more reliable nuclear deterrent. The commissioning came as the United States, South Korea and Japan began their five-day "Freedom Edge" trilateral military exercise off South Korea's Jeju island, which Kim characterized as a security threat. Experts say the Kang Kon and its sister ship, commissioned in June, were likely built with Russian assistance and are North Korea's largest and most advanced warships.

Source: The Washington Post, ABC News
