August 23, 2026

Canada vows retaliatory tariffs as trade war with US escalates

Canada said it will hit the United States with matching tariffs starting September 8, after trade talks between the two countries collapsed last week and a new 50% US duty on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods took effect. Prime Minister Mark Carney said his government's response would target US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp, paper and electronics dollar-for-dollar, and described Canada as effectively "at war" economically with its largest trading partner. No new talks are currently scheduled.

Source: Al Jazeera, NPR

Iran's security chief vows to "neutralize" US economic pressure ahead of new sanctions

Iran's top national security official, Mohsen Rezaei, threatened to halt oil flow through the Strait of Hormuz and vowed to "neutralize the economic war" as Washington prepares a sweeping new sanctions package against Tehran and any country or institution seen as offering it a financial "lifeline." President Trump has promised what he called the "most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country," while a close ally of Iran's supreme leader dismissed the rhetoric as a sign of American "desperation." The 60-day deadline for a US-Iran deal lapsed last week with no talks underway.

Source: CNN, Al Jazeera

Russia signals openness to "new ideas" on Ukraine as death toll from mall strike rises to 15

Russia's deputy foreign minister said Moscow is willing to consider new proposals to end the war in Ukraine, provided they align with President Vladimir Putin's objectives and "realities on the ground." The overture came as the death toll from a Russian double-tap drone strike on a shopping center in Kryvyi Rih, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's hometown, rose to 15 dead and 130 wounded, including children. Fighting continued elsewhere, with Russia striking the Zaporizhzhia region and Ukraine hitting an oil refinery in Perm and an airbase in Russia's Volgograd region.

Source: Al Jazeera, CBS News

Gaza disarmament roadmap stalls as Israel withholds public agreement

Mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the US finalized a roadmap for Hamas to disarm and surrender weapons in stages, part of the next phase of the Gaza ceasefire announced by President Trump. But implementation remains stuck: Israel has not publicly endorsed the latest proposal, and Hamas says it will not begin disarming until Israeli forces meet their own withdrawal obligations under the deal. Gaza's health ministry continues to report near-daily strikes despite the truce.

Source: Al Jazeera, Al Jazeera

Taiwan rehearses wartime readiness as China keeps up military pressure

Taiwan capped its annual Han Kuang exercises by briefly throttling mobile internet speeds to text-only levels in Taipei and several other cities, testing how residents would communicate if bandwidth became scarce during a Chinese attack. President Lai Ching-te apologized for the disruption, calling it "a necessary drill." The exercise came as Taiwan's defense ministry tracked dozens of Chinese warplanes and navy vessels operating around the island in a single day, and as Japan's latest defense white paper warned the military balance across the strait is "rapidly tilting in China's favor."

Source: NPR, Taipei Times

Sudan's humanitarian crisis deepens as 200,000 more displaced by war and flooding

More than 200,000 people have been newly displaced across Sudan's Kordofan region as fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces intensifies alongside seasonal flooding. Drone strikes have hit water and power infrastructure in El Obeid, and an armed attack forced Doctors Without Borders to suspend operations at a hospital in Zalingei, Darfur, amid an ongoing cholera outbreak. More than three years into the war, Sudan remains what humanitarian officials call the world's largest displacement crisis.

Source: Democracy Now!, Human Rights Watch
