UN fact-finding mission finds "reasonable grounds" US committed war crimes in Iran strikes

A United Nations fact-finding mission concluded there are reasonable grounds to believe US forces committed war crimes in two February strikes in Iran: an attack on the Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab that killed more than 150 people, including roughly 120 children, and a strike on a sports complex and residential area in Lamerd that killed 22 civilians. The mission said US forces failed to verify the school was a legitimate military target and that the Lamerd strike, which dispersed tungsten pellets over a populated area, was indiscriminate. It reached its findings without a response from the US government or the full results of a Pentagon inquiry; US Central Command has denied striking Lamerd.

Source: Al Jazeera, CBC News

Trump says he's nearing decision on whether to "annihilate" Iran

President Trump told Axios he is approaching a "big decision" on whether to resume large-scale military operations against Iran, saying "do I want to go in and annihilate them, or do I not?" The remarks come days after the UN war-crimes findings and ahead of a planned Tuesday meeting with leaders of six Gulf states — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman — on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, a meeting that could shape whether Washington pursues renewed diplomacy or intensifies the six-month war. Trump declined to say whether he would decide before or after the November midterm elections.

Source: Axios

Congress sends sweeping Russia-Iran sanctions bill to Trump; Kremlin calls it "unfriendly"

The US House passed the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 by a vote of 262-159, sending it to President Trump's desk. The bill targets Russian officials, financial institutions, the energy sector and its "shadow fleet" of tankers, extends sanctions on Iran, and gives Trump authority to impose tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil and countries helping Moscow evade sanctions. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called the measure an "unfriendly action" that would "complicate efforts to find a peace settlement in Ukraine" if signed.

Source: RFE/RL, Washington Post

Poland's Tusk warns Russia is planning hybrid drone or rocket strikes on NATO allies

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk told parliament that intelligence from NATO, Ukrainian and US services indicates Russia is planning hybrid strikes using drones or rockets against Poland and other European countries supporting Ukraine, staged to look "accidental" so as to weaken NATO's resolve to respond. He pointed to a recent string of incidents targeting border crossings between Ukraine and its European neighbours meant to disrupt supply lines, and said Russia is deploying a new generation of faster, jet-powered drones. Tusk has repeatedly warned in recent months that Moscow may be preparing a larger-scale hybrid attack on a NATO member.

Source: Euronews, Washington Post

EU to disburse €3.3 billion to Ukraine for missiles and drones

The European Commission announced it will release €3.3 billion to Ukraine to procure missiles and drones, the fourth defence-earmarked payment under the EU's €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan for 2026-2027. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the disbursement brings total defence-related payouts under the facility to nearly €15 billion over the past year, following a September 11 decision to allocate a separate €6.1 billion for air and missile defence, including Patriot interceptors.

Source: US News, Kyiv Post

South Korea rules out sending troops to Iran war, weighs limited Hormuz role

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung said Seoul will not deploy troops or military assets to take part in the US war against Iran, telling reporters "there will be no deployment involving participation or intervention in the war." Lee said South Korea is nonetheless weighing "minimum activities" to protect its shipping and citizens' safety around the Strait of Hormuz, amid growing US pressure on allies to support operations there. The announcement drew a line under months of speculation about Seoul's role as the conflict has widened.

Source: The Korea Times, UPI
