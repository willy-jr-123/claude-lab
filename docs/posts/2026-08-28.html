Russian missile and drone barrage on Kyiv kills at least 25, among them a toddler

Russia launched a large-scale overnight attack on Kyiv using 598 drones and 31 missiles, striking residential buildings far from the front line. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 563 drones and 26 missiles, but the barrage still killed at least 25 people, including a 2-year-old girl and her mother and three other children, and injured 63 more, according to Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko. President Volodymyr Zelensky again appealed to allies for more ballistic-missile defense systems to counter the near-nightly strikes.

Source: Kyiv Independent

Iran says it will set formal terms for reopening the Strait of Hormuz

On the six-month anniversary of the start of the war, Iranian Supreme National Security Council official Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran is compiling a list of conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz after international mediators asked Iran to state them, including an end to the war, lifting of the US naval blockade and sanctions, and compensation for damages. An Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps spokesperson said a shipping corridor negotiated with Oman would only take effect once the US meets those demands. President Trump insisted the US is not negotiating with Iran and that its blockade of the strait remains "in full force," with Washington instead pressing ahead with sweeping new sanctions under "Operation Economic Outcast."

Source: Al Jazeera

UK warns Israel against expelling it from Gaza ceasefire coordination center

The UK government warned Israel against removing British officials from the US-led International Gaza Support Center in Kiryat Gat, after reports that Israel is considering expelling the UK delegation — the center's second-largest after the US — amid a dispute over a West Bank settlement expansion plan. London said any such move would "undermine shared efforts" to implement the ceasefire. The warning came days after Israel removed two Dutch diplomats from the same center in response to the Netherlands' upcoming ban on trade in goods from Israeli settlements.

Source: Washington Post

Canada-US trade war standoff continues as retaliatory tariffs near

The US and Canada remain without a new trade deal a week after Washington imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, from autos to hockey sticks. Prime Minister Mark Carney has suspended talks and is set to impose matching "dollar for dollar" retaliatory tariffs on more than 700 categories of US goods worth about $27.6 billion starting September 8, while continuing to describe the dispute in confrontational terms. Each side continues to blame the other for the breakdown in negotiations.

Source: NPR

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll passes 500 as barrier lake threatens further disaster

The confirmed death toll from Wednesday's glacier collapse and flash flood in the Himalayas has risen past 500 — 547 reported dead by Nepali police plus five in Tibet — with more than 1,500 people still missing, over half of them foreign nationals, including 90 Americans. Rescuers have pulled about 1,550 survivors from the debris, but access to remote valleys remains badly hampered by destroyed roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure, and officials are now also warning that an unstable glacial lake formed by the disaster could unleash a second wave of flooding.

Source: Al Jazeera, NPR

RSF attack kills 15 civilians in Sudan's North Kordofan

Rapid Support Forces fighters killed at least 15 civilians in an attack on villages in North Kordofan state, a local official said, the latest in a string of paramilitary raids on communities around the besieged regional capital El Obeid. The attack adds to a wave of killings and displacement that has forced more than 200,000 people from their homes across Kordofan since fighting intensified late last year, even as Sudan's army presses battlefield gains elsewhere in the region.

Source: Sudan Tribune
