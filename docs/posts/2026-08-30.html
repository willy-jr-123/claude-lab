CIA chief's Moscow trip yields Trump-Putin-Zelenskyy summit proposal, but Putin balks

CIA Director John Ratcliffe proposed a trilateral summit between US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy during an unannounced trip to Moscow this week, where he met Russian intelligence chiefs Sergei Naryshkin and Alexander Bortnikov to explore a path back to talks. US officials briefed Zelenskyy on the proposal Friday; he backed it, but Putin has so far rejected the idea. The diplomatic push came as Russia kept up drone and missile attacks on Kyiv for a third straight night, while Ukraine struck the Kirishi oil refinery, the Millerovo and Yeysk airfields, and other military targets deep inside Russia.

Source: Axios, Ukrainska Pravda

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll passes 750 as more than 3,000 remain missing

The confirmed death toll from last week's Himalayan glacial lake outburst and flash flood has risen to at least 750, with 734 bodies recovered in Nepal and 16 confirmed dead on the Chinese side. Nearly 2,500 people remain missing in Nepal and about 546 more in Tibet, including dozens of foreign nationals and hundreds of Nepali hydropower workers feared trapped in flooded tunnels. Relief efforts continue as officials say the immediate threat of further flooding from the unstable glacial lake has eased.

Source: ABC News, TimesLIVE

Iran's IRGC claims "full control" of Hormuz even as Oman shipping corridor deal stalls

Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy insists its control over the Strait of Hormuz remains "complete and decisive," dismissing US claims that the waterway is open as "an obvious lie," while a separate, weeks-in-the-making agreement with Oman for a temporary 11-kilometer-wide shipping corridor through the strait has yet to take effect. Iranian officials say the strait will not fully reopen until Washington fulfills commitments under a since-lapsed interim peace deal, leaving roughly 6,000 sailors still stranded aboard ships in the Gulf.

Source: Al Jazeera, Al Jazeera

Gaza ceasefire nearing "point of no return," UN told, as strikes persist

The US-backed Board of Peace's envoy for Gaza warned the UN Security Council the ceasefire risks reaching a "point of no return" if fighting resumes, even as casualties have fallen sharply since the truce began. Violence has not stopped altogether: three Palestinians were killed and seven wounded in Israeli strikes across Gaza on August 29 alone, part of what monitors say are thousands of ceasefire violations since October. Netanyahu has publicly rejected elements of Trump's 15-point peace plan even as Hamas has pledged to surrender weapons to a new governing body.

Source: NPR, Al Jazeera

Carney says Canada is "at war" with US as retaliatory tariffs near

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney kept up combative rhetoric toward Washington, saying "you're at war when you get attacked — we got attacked," after trade talks collapsed and 50% US tariffs took effect on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods. Canada is set to impose matching, dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs on more than 700 categories of American goods starting September 8, with duties on US steel and aluminum doubling to 50%.

Source: NPR, NPR

Sudan's besieged El Obeid faces mounting humanitarian catastrophe as RSF siege drags on

North Kordofan's capital, El Obeid, remains under army control but besieged in all but name after 18 months, with roughly half a million people trapped as the Rapid Support Forces mass around the city. Relentless RSF drone strikes have knocked out power and water, food prices have surged as much as 300%, and a cholera outbreak and reports of widespread sexual violence are worsening. The UN Human Rights Council has opened an urgent inquiry into alleged abuses, with Britain warning of a risk of large-scale atrocities reminiscent of last year's fall of El Fasher.

Source: NPR, Sudan Tribune
