August 16, 2026

Ukraine launches mass drone attack on Moscow as Russia bombards Kyiv

Ukraine sent a large drone barrage toward Moscow overnight, killing at least one person and wounding three, while Moscow's mayor said around 600 drones were tracked heading toward the city and over 200 were shot down over the region. Russia responded with a ballistic missile bombardment of Kyiv, sparking fires in two districts and hitting the capital's book market, while separate strikes hit Odesa's suburbs, including a hypermarket. A NATO jet also downed a stray drone over Romanian airspace, underscoring how the war continues to spill beyond Ukraine's borders nearly five years in.

Source: Al Jazeera

Hamas chief travels to Cairo as Kushner pushes stalled Gaza roadmap

Hamas political chief Khalil al-Hayya arrived in Cairo for talks with Egypt's intelligence chief, his first visit since taking over the group's leadership, ahead of a planned trip to the region by White House envoy Jared Kushner and Board of Peace representative Nickolay Mladenov. The talks aim to push Israel toward implementing the 15-point Gaza roadmap, which has stalled since Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu rejected it earlier this month. The diplomacy came as Israeli strikes continued in Gaza and National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir drew criticism for saying he wants Gaza fully under Israeli control.

Source: Al Jazeera, The Times of Israel

Iran keeps Strait of Hormuz blockade in place as Oman framework awaits approval

The standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continued, with Iran still awaiting "final approval" on a framework worked out with Oman that would temporarily route shipping through Iranian and Omani waters before traffic shifts to a central corridor. Tehran has said the strait, effectively closed since the US-Israel war with Iran began in February, will not fully reopen until Washington ends the war and pays compensation. Attacks on vessels have continued along the alternative southern Omani route, which has absorbed most of the recent projectile strikes on shipping.

Source: CNN

Canada and US still apart on tariffs with days left before 50% duties hit

Canadian and US negotiators continued daily talks in Washington but remained short of a deal ahead of Trump's August 19 deadline to impose 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, with no carve-out for products otherwise covered by the USMCA. Ottawa has called Washington's latest offer insufficient, with sticking points including Canada's retaliatory auto tariffs, provincial alcohol restrictions and dairy quotas. A Canadian government source said Washington also wants an agreement before the deadline, but Prime Minister Mark Carney's negotiators have warned that tariffs taking effect could halt the broader trade talks.

Source: The Globe and Mail, Bloomberg

Japan's protest over Putin's Kuril Islands visit continues to reverberate

Diplomatic fallout continued over Russian President Vladimir Putin's first-ever visit to the disputed Kuril Islands, which Japan calls the Northern Territories and has claimed since they were seized by the Soviet Union at the end of World War II. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi branded the visit "absolutely unacceptable," and Tokyo's foreign ministry summoned Russia's ambassador to lodge a formal protest. The dispute remains the central obstacle preventing Japan and Russia from signing a formal peace treaty eight decades after the war's end.

Source: Al Jazeera

Iraq presses ahead with September 30 deadline for Iran-backed militias to disarm

Iraq's government is maintaining pressure on Iran-aligned militias to disarm by September 30, a deadline tied to the scheduled end of the US-led coalition mission against ISIS, with Baghdad reportedly planning to deploy army and counterterrorism units around militia operation centers once the deadline passes. The Badr Organisation, one of the largest Iran-backed factions, has warned that forced disarmament could trigger internal conflict, and only a handful of smaller groups have agreed to integrate into Iraq's official security forces so far. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has warned that militias refusing to comply face terrorism charges.

Source: The National
