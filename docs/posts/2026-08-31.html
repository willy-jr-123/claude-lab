US and Iran trade strikes in first major escalation in weeks

US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday after the IRGC was observed preparing to launch rockets carrying sea mines into the waterway, the first publicly reported direct US attack on Iranian positions in more than a month. Iran retaliated overnight with missile strikes on a US base in Jordan and on Al Minhad Air Base in the UAE; Jordan's armed forces said they intercepted and destroyed eight incoming missiles. The exchange comes six months into a standoff over control of the strait, even as US forces say they have finished clearing sea mines from its shipping lanes.

Source: The Washington Post, CNN

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll passes 900 as nearly 4,700 remain missing

The confirmed death toll from last week's Himalayan glacial lake outburst and flash flood has risen to at least 919, with 903 bodies recovered in Nepal and 16 confirmed dead on the Tibetan side of the border. More than 4,700 people are still listed as missing, the great majority of them in Nepal. The flooding struck the Rasuwa district along the Nepal-Tibet border a week ago, inundating the Trishuli River valley and burying buildings, bridges and roads.

Source: ABC News

Iceland rejects reopening EU accession talks in close referendum

Icelandic voters rejected a government push to resume long-shelved negotiations to join the European Union, with 52.8% voting no against 47.2% voting yes in Saturday's referendum. The vote does not decide membership itself but instructed the government on whether to reopen talks that began in 2009 and were suspended in 2013; concern over ceding control of Iceland's fishing waters featured heavily in the campaign. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir said her government will not pursue accession talks but will keep strengthening ties with the European Economic Area.

Source: Al Jazeera, CNN

Russian drones batter Kyiv region for a fifth straight day

Russian forces attacked Ukraine overnight with one Kh-59 cruise missile and 121 long-range attack drones; air defenses intercepted 96, but several evaded and struck 11 locations. Strikes on the Kyiv region injured six people on Sunday, three of whom were hospitalized, and damaged warehouses and a retail outlet in the Brovary district and a residential building in Boryspil. It marked a fifth consecutive day of near-constant air raid alerts over the capital.

Source: The Kyiv Independent, NPR

Israeli strike kills 3-year-old boy in Gaza as ceasefire violations continue

An Israeli strike near a bakery in Deir el-Balah on Sunday killed a three-year-old boy, Tayyem Hamdan, and a man, Hussam Nusair, Gaza medics said, with the Israeli military saying it had targeted a Hamas militant. The killing came amid what Gaza's Government Media Office says have been more than 4,300 ceasefire violations since the truce took effect in October, as the US-backed Board of Peace's disarmament plan remains stalled and warnings mount that the fragile ceasefire risks unraveling.

Source: Al Jazeera, CBC News

Sudan's besieged El Obeid remains cut off as displacement mounts

North Kordofan's capital, El Obeid, stays under Sudanese army control but effectively besieged, with roughly half a million people trapped as the Rapid Support Forces mass around the city. Fresh RSF raids on the nearby villages of Um Dareisa and Al-Shaqla killed six civilians and forced more than 1,000 people to flee on foot into El Obeid, many arriving in critical condition. The UN human rights office has warned of an impending "catastrophe," with food prices up as much as 300% amid the siege.

Source: Sudan Tribune, Al Jazeera
